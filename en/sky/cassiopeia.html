Cassiopeia

A W in the northern sky.

Best in November, around 9 p.m.No. 25 of the 88 constellations by areaBrightest star Schedar (magnitude 2.24)5 stars in this guide

The drawings on these maps are the game’s, and don’t show exact positions in the sky.

Find it in the sky

Across the Pole Star from the Big Dipper: five bright stars drawing a W. Seen all year from mid-northern latitudes.

Legend

Cassiopeia, queen of Aethiopia, boasts that her daughter Andromeda is fairer than the sea nymphs and angers Poseidon. She is set in the sky on her throne, wheeling round the Pole Star and clinging on half the year so as not to fall.

Its stars

Magnitude says how bright a star looks from here: the smaller the number, the brighter the star.

Type
Blue-white star
Distance
about 550 light years
Brightness
magnitude 2.39
Name
“Ivan” backwards: astronaut Gus Grissom’s middle name

Apollo astronauts steered by it. Its brightness changes unpredictably; the first known star of its kind.
